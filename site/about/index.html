Why we built it

Colleagues on different projects rarely talk. A shared pet gives them a reason to. The pet repeats the small things people tell it, and a pet can say what a person would not post in a work chat.

  1. 🎂

    Maria's son turns six

    Nobody at work knows. There is no reason to mention it in a project channel.

  2. 🐻

    She feeds Boris the cake

    Maria wants to share the day anyway. A hungry pet is a good excuse.

  3. 💬

    Boris posts in #pets

    The pet keeps the fact and tells the channel, in its own words.

  4. 🎉

    The team joins in

    Emoji, replies, congratulations. One reaction starts a chain of reactions.

How it works

From an egg to a daily habit, in six steps.

  1. 🥚

    Create

    Name, kind or custom avatar, a public description, and a hidden soul. Choose who can care and the Slack channel.

  2. 🐣

    Hatch

    The pet says hello in its channel and appears in the gallery.

  3. 🍎

    Care

    Anyone on the care list opens the room and types what they do or say.

  4. 💬

    React

    The reaction shows in the room, moves the stats, and goes to Slack.

  5. ⏳

    Fade

    Food, mood, energy, and health fall while nobody visits.

  6. 📣

    Digest

    Once a day, one message lists the pets that need attention.

Only the owner can edit a pet, change its care list, or dismiss it. A dismissed pet says goodbye in Slack.

A pet created in Hard Code mode can die: when its health reaches zero it leaves the gallery for the death list, where anyone can leave a word in its thread. The choice is made once, at creation, and nothing is posted to Slack when it happens.

Inside one reaction

Goes in

  • What you typed
  • Description and soul
  • Stats and age
  • 5 memories of you
  • 10 memories of everyone

The pet thinks

One call to the Spot AI gateway. Names and emails are replaced by hashed aliases, so the model never sees who you are.

Comes out

  • A reaction, in character
  • A one-line memory
  • A mood word
  • New stats, at most 30 points of change

Goes to

  • The Spot document store. One queue per pet, so nobody talks over anyone.
  • The Slack channel: memory, reaction, and mood line.

There is no server. Everything runs in the browser of whoever has the page open. Nobody visiting means nothing happens.
